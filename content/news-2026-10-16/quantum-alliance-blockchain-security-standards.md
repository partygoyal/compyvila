Nine blockchain organizations have formed the Quantum Alliance to develop shared standards for post-quantum readiness across networks, wallets, and infrastructure.

In its [founding-member announcement](https://hacken.io/network/hacken-joins-the-quantum-alliance-as-a-founding-member/), Hacken says the group will define criteria for testing quantum-resistance claims and support interoperability as networks migrate their cryptography.

## Verification alongside standards

Hacken identifies verification as its contribution: benchmarks and evidence that help teams substantiate readiness claims. An announced standards initiative is different from a completed upgrade to Bitcoin or Ethereum.

[CoinDesk’s October 16 report](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/10/16/experts-agree-quantum-computers-could-put-satoshi-s-bitcoin-at-risk-but-not-on-what-s-next) says the alliance was unveiled Thursday. It attributes an estimate of 6.4 million BTC with exposed public keys to the group, while stressing that existing quantum computers cannot steal those coins through the proposed attack today.

## The unresolved questions

Timelines and the treatment of vulnerable coins remain disputed. Any Bitcoin security transition would require coordination across the ecosystem, rather than a unilateral decision by the alliance.
