A stablecoin depeg occurs when its traded price moves away from its intended reference value, such as one US dollar. A price target is a design objective. It does not ensure that every holder can sell or redeem every token at that value at every moment.

## Market price and redemption are different

The price on an exchange is the amount buyers and sellers currently accept there. Redemption is a separate process governed by an issuer or protocol’s rules. Access, settlement timing, costs, and eligibility can differ from ordinary trading.

Circle describes USDC as redeemable one-for-one and explains institutional access through [its USDC product information](https://www.circle.com/usdc). Its [redemption structure documentation](https://help.circle.com/support/en/usdc-eurc-redemption-structure?id=kb_article_view&sysparm_article=KB0010644&sysparm_language=en) provides further detail. A retail holder trading through an exchange should not assume they have the same direct redemption access as an eligible issuer customer.

## Why deviations can develop

A price can move when holders want to exit faster than available counterparties or redemption channels can absorb the flow. Reserve concerns, operational interruptions, banking access, collateral price changes, and protocol design can each matter. The [BIS study on stablecoin policy issues](https://www.bis.org/fsi/publ/insights57.pdf) examines reserve, liquidity, and redemption risks.

Different stabilization designs create different exposures. A token backed by conventional reserves, a token secured by crypto collateral, and a token relying on algorithmic incentives should not be evaluated using one identical reserve checklist. Start by identifying the actual mechanism.

## A hypothetical depeg example

Imagine a dollar-targeting token trades at $0.97 on one venue. Selling 1,000 tokens at that quoted price would produce $970 before fees, provided enough liquidity exists for the full order. That is a $30 difference from the intended $1,000 reference value.

Now suppose an eligible institution can redeem at $1, but must pay fees and wait for settlement. Buying at $0.97 and redeeming might look attractive, yet profitability depends on access, execution costs, delays, and the possibility that conditions change. The visible discount alone does not establish a risk-free opportunity for every holder.

These invented numbers explain the distinction between a market discount and redemption value. They are not a current quote or a claim about any particular token.

## What reserve disclosures can tell you

Look for the reporting date, assets covered, liabilities considered, methodology, and independent assurance language. Circle’s [transparency page](https://www.circle.com/transparency) publishes reserve information and related reporting. An issuer’s disclosure is evidence to examine, rather than a guarantee against every future trading or operational problem.

A statement about reserves at a past reporting date is not a continuous view of all market conditions. A credible review distinguishes the reserve evidence from assumptions about immediate liquidity, legal rights, and access through an intermediary.

## A checklist for reading a depeg headline

1. Identify the token contract, network, and issuer or protocol. Similar tickers are not proof of identical assets.
2. Record the venue, pair, observed price, timestamp, and available trading depth.
3. Compare other credible venues before treating a single anomalous trade as a market-wide price.
4. Check official issuer or protocol notices for interruptions and the stated redemption process.
5. Separate verified facts from rumors and preserve links to original statements.
6. Recheck conditions before acting; a screenshot can become outdated quickly.

This is a research process rather than a prediction model. A short-lived deviation and a persistent loss of confidence can initially share similar symptoms. A universal percentage threshold or recovery deadline would obscure the differences.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does every stablecoin target one dollar?

No. The reference can be another currency or asset. Identify the intended peg before interpreting a price move.

### Does a small deviation mean collapse?

Not by itself. The size, duration, venue liquidity, redemption status, and mechanism all affect the interpretation. A small deviation is also not proof of safety.

### Will a depegged stablecoin always recover?

There is no universal recovery guarantee. Outcomes depend on the design, resources, operations, and market conditions. Past recoveries do not establish what a different token will do.

### Is holding through an exchange the same as direct redemption?

No. Your practical exit can depend on the exchange’s trading, withdrawal, and account rules as well as the token’s own redemption arrangements.
